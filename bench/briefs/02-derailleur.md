# Bench brief 02: how a rear derailleur moves the chain between sprockets

**Kind:** accuracy-heavy mechanism. The shape is easy to get wrong; this brief is judged on the checklist below.
**Duration: 25-40 s**
**Formats:** 16x9 and 9x16, one timeline.
**Audio:** procedural score only. No narration, no voice. On-screen text carries the words.
**Audience:** a rider who clicks the shifter every day and has never looked at what happens at the back wheel.

## Source facts (use these; no web research needed)

- A derailleur bike drives the rear wheel with a chain from the **chainring** (at the pedals) to one of several **sprockets** stacked on the rear hub. The stack is called the **cassette**; 8 to 12 sprockets are common.
- The sprockets on the cassette get **smaller toward the outside** of the bike. The largest sprocket sits innermost, next to the spokes. The smallest sits outermost.
- A **larger** rear sprocket is an **easier** (lower) gear, for climbing. A smaller one is a harder (higher) gear, for speed.
- The **rear derailleur** hangs from the frame just behind and below the rear axle (on the dropout, via a hanger). It carries a **cage** with two small toothed wheels, the **pulleys**: the **upper (guide) pulley** sits close under the cassette, the **lower (tension) pulley** sits below it.
- **Chain path, seen from the drive side:** the **top run** of the chain goes straight from the top of the chainring to the top of the rear sprocket and is the taut, driving side. The chain wraps around the back of the sprocket, comes down to the upper pulley, runs through the cage to the lower pulley in an S-shaped path, and the **bottom run** goes forward from the lower pulley to the bottom of the chainring.
- A **parallelogram linkage** moves the cage sideways, parallel to the axle, while keeping the pulleys upright and in line with the chain. It does not tilt the cage sideways.
- The **shifter cable** pulls the parallelogram **inward** toward the larger sprockets (easier gear). Releasing the cable lets a **spring** pull it **outward** toward the smaller sprockets.
- The cage is also spring-loaded to **rotate** around its top pivot and take up chain slack: on a larger sprocket the chain needs more length, so the cage swings **forward**; on a smaller sprocket it swings back and keeps the chain tight.
- A shift only happens **while the pedals turn forward**. The upper pulley guides the chain sideways, and over part of a turn the sprocket's teeth pick it up. The chain moves one sprocket per click; it never jumps instantly.

## Accuracy checklist (a correct film shows every one)

1. Cassette sprockets get smaller toward the outside; the largest is next to the spokes.
2. The derailleur hangs below and behind the rear axle, attached to the frame.
3. Two pulleys in a cage: the upper one close under the cassette, the lower one below it.
4. Top run of the chain goes straight from the top of the chainring to the top of the sprocket.
5. The chain wraps the back of the sprocket, runs through both pulleys in an S-shape, and the bottom run returns to the bottom of the chainring.
6. A parallelogram moves the cage sideways, parallel to the axle; the pulleys stay upright.
7. Cable pull moves the chain inward to a larger sprocket (easier gear); release lets the spring move it outward to a smaller one.
8. On a larger sprocket the cage swings forward to feed more chain; on a smaller one it swings back.
9. The shift happens while the pedals turn forward, over part of a turn, not as an instant jump. The turning crank is on screen during the shift; a caption alone does not count.
10. Larger sprocket is labelled or shown as the easier gear, smaller as the harder gear.
11. Relative sizes are true in every view: the smallest rear sprocket is clearly smaller than the chainring (about a quarter of its diameter on a road bike), the wheel is far larger than either, and a sprocket has the same size in the side and rear views.

## What the film must show

- A side view (drive side) that makes the chain path readable, and a view from behind or above that makes the sideways movement readable.
- At least one shift up and one shift down, each tied to a click on the shifter.
- The words "easier" and "harder" (or "climb" and "speed") on screen when the chain lands on a sprocket.
