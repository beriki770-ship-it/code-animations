# Bench brief 03: eight billion, and how fast we got there

**Kind:** data film. A chart that builds on screen; every number appears when the film reaches it.
**Duration: 20-30 s**
**Formats:** 16x9 and 9x16, one timeline.
**Audio:** procedural score only. No narration, no voice. A sound lands on every number as it appears.
**Audience:** a general feed viewer who knows the world is "about 8 billion" and not how recent that is.

## Source facts (use these; no web research needed)

World population milestones, as commonly given from UN estimates (years are approximate):

| Milestone | Year reached | Years since the previous billion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 billion | 1804 | - |
| 2 billion | 1927 | 123 |
| 3 billion | 1960 | 33 |
| 4 billion | 1974 | 14 |
| 5 billion | 1987 | 13 |
| 6 billion | 1999 | 12 |
| 7 billion | 2011 | 12 |
| 8 billion | 2022 | 11 |

The headline: the first billion took all of human history up to 1804; the step from 7 to 8 billion took 11 years.

## What the film must show

- A chart with a true time axis (years to scale, 1800 to 2025), so the gap 1804-1927 looks long and the recent gaps look short. Don't space the milestones evenly.
- Each milestone appears in order with its year and its "years since" number on screen, landing on a sound.
- The 123 and the 11 are the two numbers the viewer must remember; they get the most screen time.
- Every number on screen matches the table above exactly.
